# AI-enabled builders — working category definition

This is a draft for public writing and job curation, based on the user's role judgments throughout the exploration. It defines the work, not a required job title. The name of the category is provisional.

## The short definition

**An AI-enabled builder uses AI to personally turn a real problem into a working solution, puts that solution in people's hands, and takes responsibility for whether it works.**

These people may come from product, operations, design, software engineering, or another discipline. What unites them is ownership of a shorter loop between understanding a problem and delivering a useful result. AI helps them do more of that work directly, with fewer handoffs. They still need judgment about users, quality, limits, and when to bring in specialists.

The underlying **builder archetype predates AI coding tools**. A staff software engineer working on an internal platform can fit it by finding developer friction, deciding what capability would help, building it, and seeing whether teams adopt it. AI is not what makes that person a builder. It is what may now let more people, including those without an engineering career, own a similar loop. This project concentrates on that emerging expansion, especially product-focused roles; it does not need to treat established staff SWE roles as its main job-discovery audience.

The primary role this project seeks is the **product-focused AI-enabled builder**: someone who decides what is worth building for users, builds directly with AI, and improves the result based on use. The most distinctive case is a product-minded person who ships working software with AI without needing a prior career in software engineering. A product-minded engineer can also be a strong fit; engineering experience is a qualification dimension, not the definition of the category.

## What is new in this wave

Product work has often been split across the person identifying a need, the person designing a solution, and the person implementing it. AI coding and building tools let one person carry more of that loop. The point is not simply faster prototypes or fewer engineers. It is tighter contact between the person making product decisions and the working thing users experience. A builder can test an idea, change it, and learn from the result without every step becoming a handoff.

That shift makes a different set of strengths valuable together: problem judgment, initiative, technical resourcefulness, the ability to work with AI tools, and responsibility for outcomes. The role may be senior or technically demanding. "No software-engineering career required" does not mean "no technical skill required."

## What makes a role a match

Look for the **overall mandate**, not a title or one sentence about Claude Code. A strong product-focused match shows all of these:

An explicit role-wide statement combining product judgment, domain understanding and AI-native execution is meaningful evidence when supported by personal-building requirements. Read opening paragraphs, responsibilities and qualifications together. Ordinary PM duties do not negate that combined mandate, and the employer need not repeat "personally write production code" in each section. A generic builder label or AI-tool mention alone is still insufficient. The user's reaffirmation of Apollo on October 29 clarifies this evidence-weighting rule.

1. **Problem and product judgment.** The person understands users or employees, chooses what to build, makes tradeoffs, and can change direction based on evidence.
2. **Personal building as central work.** The person makes working software, agents, automations, tools, features, or other usable artifacts themselves. The work is more than a mockup or occasional supporting prototype. It need not mean owning every production system alone: engineering review, infrastructure, and collaboration are compatible with the role.
3. **AI used to build.** AI materially helps the person implement, test, or iterate on the solution. The result does not have to be an AI product.
4. **Delivery and learning.** The person gets the solution into use or meaningful testing, observes whether it helps, and remains involved in improving it. Building something impressive in isolation is not enough.

The first three are the category's core signals. Delivery and learning clarify what "builder" means in practice; a posting can be a plausible match when the destination of the work is implied rather than spelled out, but we should show that uncertainty.

**Problem ownership is distinct from technical ownership.** A role should involve understanding users or business workflows, investigating and framing needs, prioritizing opportunities, or helping decide what to build and why. This can be shared and scoped; it need not mean owning the company roadmap. Maintaining integrations, choosing architecture, fixing assigned issues or owning a technical component does not automatically establish it. Internal developers count as users, so platform engineers can qualify when that broader responsibility is present. If direct AI-assisted building is clear but the problem-understanding mandate is genuinely unresolved, retain Maybe/unclear. The user's T07 PlanetScale feedback is uncertain, not a categorical rejection of engineers or developer-experience roles.

## What "focus" means

**Focus means the main kind of decision and outcome the person is accountable for in this job.** It does not mean their prior profession, the tools they use, or simply the artifact they make. A former engineer can have a product focus; a PM can have an operations focus; either might build an agent. Read the responsibilities and success measures to identify the focus, then read the qualifications separately to identify the experience gate.

Product is the primary focus of this project, not the only valid one. Focuses can overlap, and new ones may emerge.

| Center of responsibility | Main question the builder owns | Example from the user's judgments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | What should users have, and is the resulting experience working? | Axiad, Medecision, Overjoy, Veho |
| Operations | Which business process should change, and does the built system improve it? | Rillet, RK Logistics Group |
| Design | What should the experience be, and can the designer build and refine it directly? | Babylist |
| Engineering-led overlap | What technical capability should be built for a real user problem, and does it work in use? | TRM Labs, Sekai |

These are examples of the reviewed roles, not claims that the postings are still open. The engineering-led label needs a stricter explanation because building software with AI is already ordinary engineering work. **Engineering work alone does not establish this category.** An engineering-led builder also has meaningful responsibility for understanding a user or business problem, shaping the solution rather than only implementing assigned requirements, and learning from the working result. In the user-confirmed TRM example, the person sits with mission users, builds integrations, pipelines, and prototypes for their needs, and carries that context into the product roadmap. Sekai is a product-centric engineer who uses AI as a core part of building. These roles are relevant overlaps, though they may be less accessible to the project's priority non-engineering audience.

There is no perfectly sharp line between an excellent product-minded software engineer and an engineering-led builder. Staff SWE roles that own internal-platform problems from discovery through adoption can already fit the underlying archetype. For the current **AI-enabled** collection, also look for AI as a material building tool. A posting that only assigns features and asks an engineer to implement them with AI tools does not show the same problem-to-outcome ownership. A forward-deployed role can qualify when it builds for users and carries what it learns into product direction; customer-specific implementation alone does not establish that.

## Keep the entry requirement separate

For each role, read the qualifications first and state which of these applies:

- **Open to non-engineering backgrounds:** demonstrated building and technical fluency matter, but a prior software-engineering career is not required. This is the priority audience fit.
- **Engineering-background blend:** the role truly combines judgment and direct AI-assisted building, but asks for substantial professional engineering experience. Keep it visible as a different type.
- **Unclear or implied gate:** the requirements are ambiguous, or the work seems to demand an engineer despite broader language. Explain the uncertainty.

Also state seniority and domain requirements rather than treating every demanding role as a mismatch. Alt and SharkNinja remain fits in the user's assessment despite high requirements. A requirement to write production code in the *new role* is not automatically a requirement for a previous software-engineering career.

## Boundaries that matter

- **Traditional PM or AI-native PM:** Using AI for research, specs, strategy, and prototypes does not qualify when engineers still own the central building and delivery work. The user rejected Legion Health, CloudZero, and the Rillet PM posting on this distinction; the user was uncertain about SevenAI because personal building was not clear.
- **AI transformation owner:** Leading an AI roadmap or coordinating squads does not by itself make someone a builder. The user rejected the reviewed RWE role for this reason.
- **Conventional software engineer using AI:** Coding faster with AI does not by itself create the broader builder role. The user rejected the reviewed brightwheel and Docker postings because they required substantial professional engineering experience. That judgment does not exclude all engineers: engineering-focused builders can qualify when they directly connect real user needs to what they build, as with TRM and Sekai.
- **Solutions or implementation role:** Personally building for customers does not automatically imply product ownership or a broader builder mandate. The user rejected Cyvl and Form Energy as solutions-focused, while accepting TRM because it builds for mission users and brings their needs into the product roadmap.
- **A demanding hybrid role:** High expectations alone do not disqualify a role. The distinction is whether the person owns meaningful problem decisions and directly builds with AI; the experience gate should be shown honestly.
